Calculations

Heat Duty Q = m · Cp · ΔT / 3600 [kW], computed independently for hot and cold streams. Q_avg = (Q_hot + Q_cold) / 2 is used for U and R_f calcs.

LMTD for counter-current flow: (ΔT₁ − ΔT₂) / ln(ΔT₁ / ΔT₂), where ΔT₁ = T_h_in − T_c_out and ΔT₂ = T_h_out − T_c_in. When ΔT₁ ≈ ΔT₂ within 1×10⁻⁹ the dashboard falls back to ΔT₁ to avoid division by near-zero.

F-factor calculated from the TEMA general N-shell-pass formula using the configured number of shell passes (assumes 2N tube passes). Handles the R = 1 special case (atanh form) and the general case with iterated P1 substitution. Returns null if a temperature crossover is detected (P ≥ 1 or PR ≥ 1).

Overall U = Q_avg × 1000 / (A × F × LMTD) [W/m²K].

Fouling Rf = 1/U_dirty − 1/U_clean [m²K/W]. Reported × 10⁴. Negative R_f means U_dirty exceeds U_clean (e.g. just after cleaning); shown as-is, not clamped.

Effectiveness ε = Q_avg / (C_min × ΔT_max) × 100 [%], where C = m·Cp/3600 (kW/K) and ΔT_max = T_h_in − T_c_in.

Q imbalance = |Q_hot − Q_cold| / Q_avg × 100 [%]. Energy balance check.

Duty vs design = (Q_avg − Q_design) / Q_design × 100 [%], shown next to the Heat Duty KPI.

P, R dimensionless: P = (T_c_out − T_c_in) / (T_h_in − T_c_in); R = (T_h_in − T_h_out) / (T_c_out − T_c_in). Used inside the F-factor calc and surfaced in the Data & Log tab.

Alert tiers

Alarm

  • Rf ≥ alarm threshold (default 10×10⁻⁴ m²K/W): clean schedule trigger.
  • Duty deviation ≥ alarm percent (default 10 %).
  • Temperature crossover detected (F-factor calc fails).

Advisory

  • Rf ≥ advisory threshold (default 5×10⁻⁴).
  • Duty deviation ≥ advisory percent (default 5 %).
  • Effectiveness below advisory floor (default 50 %).
  • Q imbalance above advisory limit (default 8 %).
  • Either approach (hot end or cold end) below minimum (default 10 °C).

Series conventions

All series are continuous lines. Reference, design, and alarm lines are dashed. Chart line breaks at any timestamp where the underlying sensor returns no value, so a gap is missing data, not zero.

Scope

Single counter-current shell-and-tube exchanger, daily aggregated readings. Tracks fouling, U, duty, effectiveness, and approach temperatures against design and alarm bounds. Not a control system.

Engineering Manual - Shell & Tube Exchanger

1. Purpose & Scope

This dashboard tracks fouling and thermal performance of a single counter-current shell-and-tube heat exchanger from daily process readings. It computes the overall heat-transfer coefficient U, the fouling resistance Rf, and the LMTD-method correction factor F for the configured shell-pass count, and benchmarks delivered duty against the design value.

The dashboard does not perform mechanical design (tube count, baffle spacing, shell-side bypass) and is not a control system. It is a performance monitor for steady-state operation against a clean-condition baseline.

2. Equipment Context

Geometry: N shell passes, 2N tube passes (TEMA E or F construction). N is configured in the Data & Configuration panel; default N = 1 (one shell, two tube passes), which is the most common refinery service.

Hot stream: shell side. Heavy hydrocarbon, refinery vacuum bottoms, or other high-temperature process fluid being cooled.

Cold stream: tube side. Heat-transfer fluid (Dowtherm, hot oil, BFW) absorbing process heat for re-use upstream.

Reference dataset: 71 daily samples of a vacuum-bottoms / Dowtherm Q exchanger spanning June 2020 - January 2021. Includes a startup transient and a mid-period clean.

3. Operating Envelope

Hot inlet: 150 - 340 °C. Heavy-hydrocarbon service, vacuum bottoms or comparable.

Hot outlet: 115 - 260 °C. Cooled by 50 - 110 °C across the bundle.

Cold inlet: 100 - 190 °C. Heat-transfer fluid return from the user system.

Cold outlet: 120 - 290 °C. Limited by hot-side outlet temperature and by cold-side flow.

Hot mass flow: 4,000 - 55,000 kg/hr. Typical 35,000 - 50,000 kg/hr.

Cold mass flow: 16,000 - 55,000 kg/hr. Typical 50,000 - 55,000 kg/hr.

Cp values: hot Cp 2.1 - 2.7 kJ/(kg·K) (heavy hydrocarbon, T-dependent); cold Cp 1.9 - 2.2 kJ/(kg·K) (heat-transfer fluid).

5. Calculations

5.1 Heat duty. Independent first-law balance on each stream:

Q_hot = m_hot · cp_hot · (T_h,in - T_h,out) / 3600

Q_cold = m_cold · cp_cold · (T_c,out - T_c,in) / 3600

Q_avg = (Q_hot + Q_cold) / 2 (kW)

The /3600 converts the kg/hr × kJ/kg·K × °C product from kJ/hr to kW. Q_avg is used downstream for U and Rf; Q_hot and Q_cold individually are kept for the imbalance check and the duty trend chart.

5.2 Energy imbalance.

Imbalance % = |Q_hot - Q_cold| / Q_avg × 100

Field instruments rarely close the heat balance to better than 2-3 %. Above 8 % an advisory fires - flow meter calibration, Cp assumption, or one-side measurement drift are the usual culprits.

5.3 LMTD (counter-current).

ΔT₁ = T_h,in - T_c,out

ΔT₂ = T_h,out - T_c,in

LMTD = (ΔT₁ - ΔT₂) / ln(ΔT₁ / ΔT₂)

If ΔT₁ ≈ ΔT₂ within 1×10⁻⁹ the calc returns ΔT₁ (the limit value), avoiding the log-of-one division by zero. If either ΔT is non-positive a temperature crossover has occurred and the row is marked alarm; F-factor and U are not computed for that row but the batch continues.

5.4 P and R ratios.

P = (T_c,out - T_c,in) / (T_h,in - T_c,in)

R = (T_h,in - T_h,out) / (T_c,out - T_c,in)

Both are dimensionless inputs to the F-factor formula. P is the cold-side temperature rise as a fraction of the maximum possible. R is the heat-capacity-rate ratio (cold over hot). Values surfaced in the Data & Log table for diagnostic use.

5.5 F-factor (TEMA general N-shell-pass formula). Implements the cheguide.com / Bowman-Mueller-Nagle correction for an exchanger with N shell passes and 2N tube passes.

Special case R = 1 (balanced flow): solve for P1 = P / (N - P·(N-1)), then S = P1·√2 / (2 - P1), then F = P1·√2 / ((2 - P1) · atanh(S)) using the stable log form of atanh.

General case R ≠ 1: compute X = ((1 - PR)/(1 - P))^(1/N) and P1 = (1 - X) / (R - X), then evaluate the standard 1-shell formula on (P1, R) using A = √(R² + 1) and the two log terms B and C.

Guard rails: returns null on temperature crossover (P ≥ 1 or PR ≥ 1), on root-of-negative or log-of-non-positive arguments, and on N ≤ 0. Numerically delicate near P = 1; the EPSILON checks at 1×10⁻⁹ trip when denominators collapse and the row is marked accordingly.

5.6 Overall U.

U_dirty = Q_avg × 1000 / (A · F · LMTD) (W/m²K)

The ×1000 converts Q_avg from kW to W. U falls as the bundle fouls.

5.7 Fouling resistance.

R_f = 1/U_dirty - 1/U_clean (m²K/W, displayed × 10⁴)

Rf can go negative just after a clean if U_dirty briefly exceeds U_clean (the clean baseline is a long-term reference, not the post-clean peak). The dashboard does not clamp negative Rf; the value is informative.

5.8 Effectiveness.

C_hot = m_hot · cp_hot / 3600 (kW/K)

C_cold = m_cold · cp_cold / 3600 (kW/K)

Q_max = min(C_hot, C_cold) · (T_h,in - T_c,in) (kW)

ε = Q_avg / Q_max × 100 (%)

ε reads how close the exchanger is to its thermodynamic ceiling for the given inlet temperatures and capacity rates. A drop in ε with U and Rf stable usually indicates a flow or temperature-driven envelope shift, not fouling.

5.9 Duty vs design.

Δ = (Q_avg - Q_design) / Q_design × 100 (%)

Surfaced as the green/red diff next to the Heat Duty KPI tile. Default thresholds: ±5 % advisory, ±10 % alarm. A persistent under-duty with Rf climbing is the classic clean-schedule prompt.

6. Status Logic

Status is rolled up from individual checks. Order of escalation: alarm overrides advisory overrides OK.

Alarm

  • Rf at or above the alarm threshold (default 10×10⁻⁴ m²K/W).
  • Duty deviation at or above the alarm percent (default 10 %).
  • Temperature crossover detected on the row (F-factor calc returned null).

Advisory

  • Rf at or above the advisory threshold (default 5×10⁻⁴ m²K/W).
  • Duty deviation at or above the advisory percent (default 5 %).
  • Effectiveness ε below the advisory floor (default 50 %).
  • Q imbalance above the advisory limit (default 8 %).
  • Either approach below the minimum (default 10 °C).

7. Pressure Convention

This dashboard does not include pressure-based limits in the standard build. If the source data carries shell-side or tube-side pressure-drop tags (kPa or bar), the engine accepts them via the optional shell_dp_kpa and tube_dp_kpa aliases for export, but they are not evaluated by the alert logic. Pressure-drop trending is appropriate when fouling is being diagnosed against a known clean baseline DP, which is out of scope for this single-equipment view.

All temperatures are in °C. Internally the LMTD calculation works in temperature differences so the °C / K choice cancels.

8. Series Conventions on Charts

All measured series are continuous lines. Design, advisory, and alarm reference lines are dashed: design in neutral gray, advisory in amber (#C4890E), alarm in red (#D4432A). Chart series colors are restricted to the gold and supporting palette - red (#D4432A) is reserved for status semantics and never used as a normal data line.

9. Time Range Behavior

The topbar pill bar (1H, 24H, 7D, 30D, 90D) and the inline custom range (number + unit) drive both the trend chart x-axis and the Performance Averages KPI row. The Current Values row is unaffected by the range and always reflects the latest reading in the dataset.

The chart x-axis is padded to the full requested window. If the dataset is shorter than the window, the older portion shows as blank space (the line drops to nothing) rather than being compressed onto a shorter axis.

Window cutoff is computed off the latest dataset timestamp, not off wall-clock time. Demo data and historical CSV uploads behave the same way.

10. Data Quality & Limitations

Per-row Cp values are taken at face value from the input feed. In real service Cp is temperature-dependent; the demo data uses spot-evaluated Cp at each row's temperature, which is the cleanest assumption for a shell-and-tube tracking tool.

Mass flow values can carry ±2 - 5 % uncertainty depending on meter type (orifice vs Coriolis vs ultrasonic). The Q imbalance check at 8 % is set to absorb typical instrumentation noise without firing on every row.

The engine assumes single-phase fluids on both sides. Phase change (vaporization, condensation) violates the m·Cp·ΔT formulation and will produce wildly biased Q values. Reboiler and condenser duty tracking needs a different model.

The F-factor calc assumes N shell passes with 2N tube passes (TEMA E with longitudinal baffle). For TEMA G, H, J, X or other non-standard geometries the formula does not apply; F should be entered manually or U computed without the F correction.

11. Common Fault Modes

U falling, Rf climbing, hot outlet rising: classical fouling. Schedule a clean when Rf crosses the alarm threshold or when duty deviation passes 10 %.

Q imbalance jumping but U/Rf stable: instrument drift. Likely candidates: flow meter calibration on the side that moved, Cp assumption changing with composition, or sensor placement issues.

Effectiveness ε falling with U flat: envelope shift, not fouling. The hot inlet may have dropped or the flow ratio changed - check upstream feed conditions before scheduling work.

Temperature crossover (row alarm, F-factor null): impossible thermodynamically for a single-pass counter-current; almost always an instrumentation fault, a swap of inlet and outlet sensors, or a bypass leak. Check sensor labels first.

Rf goes negative after a maintenance window: the bundle is performing better than the U_clean reference. Update the U_clean baseline to the post-clean stabilized value and re-evaluate.
