Worked Example: LPG and Naphthathe full arithmetic, checkable by hand

These two products are priced in two stages. The Government of Alberta publishes a reference price each month, in Canadian dollars per cubic metre, which sets the level. That figure lags about two months, so it is rolled forward to today using a daily US benchmark: the Alberta price is scaled by how far that benchmark has moved since the Alberta month's own average.

No exchange rate appears anywhere in this calculation. The Alberta price is already Canadian, and the daily benchmark enters as a ratio of two US prices, which cancels out. Applying a rate to either would count it twice.

How the Crude Feed Is Pricedtwo stages, kept separate on purpose
1 WCS = WTI + the WCS–WTI differential
2 feed price = WCS + that feed's grade differential
3 C$/m³ = US$/bbl × exchange rate × 6.28981077

Alberta publishes the differential monthly and as a positive discount; the model takes it as negative, since WCS trades below WTI. WTI itself is a daily price, so the pair together give a daily WCS estimate rather than a month-old one. The exchange rate is applied once, at the very end, and never inside the differential arithmetic.

Grade differentials are a calibration snapshot rather than market assessments. One feed matches a named published stream exactly; the rest are proxies. The Data & Log tab shows which is which for each feed.

Known Limitationswhat these prices are not

These are bulk values at the refinery gate. No pump prices, no consumer fuel tax, no retail margin. The model represents the economics of the refinery, not of the fuel retail chain.

The US benchmarks are Gulf Coast prices. This refinery is in Western Canada, so a real basis difference exists between the two markets. No basis adjustment is applied, because there is no defensible figure to apply. The two Alberta-anchored products avoid this, since their anchor is already an Alberta price.

Butane has no daily published price. Its Alberta anchor is real, but the daily movement borrows the gasoline benchmark. That is the weakest link in the product pricing and it affects LPG.

Pentanes plus is condensate, not a refinery naphtha cut. It remains a proxy for naphtha, though a much closer one than finished gasoline, which is a blended consumer fuel and trades well above a blendstock.

Unconverted oil has no live price at all. The US stopped publishing residual fuel oil prices in 2022, so it is valued as a fraction of the heavy crude price. That fraction is the first thing to revisit if a heavy-product assessment becomes available.

Every figure here is an estimate. None of it is a market assessment, and none of it should be used to settle a transaction.

What this model computes

Crude to Profit prices a five-crude refinery blend from feedstock to finished product. Five Canadian crude streams blend into one feed, split on a per-crude assay yield into primary distillation cuts, and the two heavy cuts convert further: vacuum residue through a residue hydrocracker, and the leftover unconverted oil through a hydrocracker/hydrotreater. The resulting seven-product slate is priced against the crude feed cost at a low and a high price case, in C$/hr and million C$/yr.

This is a margin screening tool. It does not optimize the blend, respect unit capacity limits, or check that a product meets specification. It answers what this blend earns at these prices for a fixed configuration, not what blend to buy.

Calculation chain

Six stages run in order. Crude blend combines the five feeds and reports each crude's share, sulfur load, and flow in barrels. Primary distillation applies each crude's assay yield table to split it into naphtha, AGO, VGO, vacuum residue, and the three VGO sub-cuts (LVGO, MVGO, HVGO). Residue Hydrocracker converts the vacuum residue into naphtha, a diesel cut, three VGO grades, and an unconverted bottoms stream, on a mass balance basis at a fixed feed and product density. Each intermediate stream then splits into the seven finished cuts on its own per-stream split table. The unconverted oil left over from that split becomes the hydrocracker/hydrotreater feed and splits again on its own yield table, where LPG is the C3 plus iC4 plus nC4 roll-up. Economics sums crude cost and product revenue at a low and a high price case and reports the difference as gross margin, annualised at 24 hours a day and 330 operating days a year.

Cut point: a diagnostic, not a driver

The dashboard reports a cut point of 367°C as a fixed, read-only figure. In the source model this number only feeds a display column. It does not change the product slate; the finished-product volumes come entirely from the per-stream split tables in the Data & Log tab. Treat the cut point as a reference for how well the split assumption matches a real distillation curve, not as a control that shifts output.

Difference against the original spreadsheet

This model routes the naphtha stream differently from the spreadsheet it was built from, which lifts the margin by roughly 39 %. The volume balance closes exactly here: everything fed into the product split comes back out. Worth walking through together before the figures are used.

Live prices and data sources

Crude and product prices come from two public sources, and the panel on the Dashboard tab shows the publication date of every figure it uses. The Government of Alberta publishes Western Canadian Select, WTI, and the spread between them. The U.S. Energy Information Administration publishes daily spot prices for crude and refined products. The exchange rate is fetched daily.

Crude is priced in two steps. A daily WCS is estimated first, from WTI and the published WCS-WTI spread, because the spread moves slowly while the outright price tracks WTI day to day. Each feed is then priced as WCS plus a differential for its grade, and the result converts to Canadian dollars per cubic metre once, at the end. The crude table lists the grade each feed is priced against.

Products are valued four ways. Kerosene and diesel have real traded benchmarks, against Gulf Coast jet fuel and ULSD. LPG and naphtha are anchored on the Government of Alberta’s monthly NGL reference prices, which are Alberta prices already quoted in Canadian dollars per cubic metre, and rolled forward to today by a daily US benchmark. The two swing cuts and unconverted oil have no external price at all and are calculated instead. The Sources & Formulas tab shows the arithmetic for each.

A swing cut is worth whatever pool it is sent to, so its value is derived rather than looked up. The 128 to 145 C cut is valued between the naphtha and kerosene pools, and the 340 to 385 C cut between unconverted oil and the diesel pool. The split between destinations is a model setting. Unconverted oil is valued as a fraction of the heavy crude price, because no live heavy-product benchmark is published any more.

Every figure is a bulk value at the refinery gate. None of it includes consumer fuel tax, retail markup, or station margin, and no pump prices are used anywhere. The model represents the economics of the refinery, not of the fuel-retail chain. The benchmarks are US market prices while the refinery is in Western Canada, so a real basis difference remains and is deliberately left at zero rather than guessed at.

All of these are estimates drawn from benchmarks, not assessed prices for these specific streams. The model runs on the fixed reference prices in the Data and Log tab unless live prices are applied deliberately, so the headline margin does not move on its own.

Scope and limitations

This engine prices one blend at one set of yields. It is volumetrically additive with no shrinkage correction on blending, and the Residue Hydrocracker step converts every product on a single generic density (862 kg/m³) rather than a per-product one, matching the source workbook's own simplification. Utilities, hydrogen, catalyst, labour, and capital charges sit outside the boundary; the reported figure is a gross crude-to-product margin, not a net cash margin. The 330 operating days a year is an availability assumption, not a measurement. Crude density and sulfur are fixed reference values from assay data, not live feeds.
