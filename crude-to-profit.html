What this model computes

Crude to Profit prices a five-crude refinery blend from feedstock to finished product. Five Canadian crude streams blend into one feed, split on a per-crude assay yield into primary distillation cuts, and the two heavy cuts convert further: vacuum residue through an LC-Fining resid hydrocracker, and the leftover unconverted oil through a hydrocracker/hydrotreater. The resulting seven-product slate is priced against the crude feed cost at a low and a high price case, in C$/hr and million C$/yr.

This is a margin screening tool. It does not optimize the blend, respect unit capacity limits, or check that a product meets specification. It answers what this blend earns at these prices for a fixed configuration, not what blend to buy.

Calculation chain

Six stages run in order. Crude blend combines the five feeds and reports each crude's share, sulfur load, and flow in barrels. Primary distillation applies each crude's assay yield table to split it into naphtha, AGO, VGO, vacuum residue, and the three VGO sub-cuts (LVGO, MVGO, HVGO). LC-Fining converts the vacuum residue into naphtha, a diesel cut, three VGO grades, and an unconverted bottoms stream, on a mass balance basis at a fixed feed and product density. Each intermediate stream then splits into the seven finished cuts on its own per-stream split table. The unconverted oil left over from that split becomes the hydrocracker/hydrotreater feed and splits again on its own yield table, where LPG is the C3 plus iC4 plus nC4 roll-up. Economics sums crude cost and product revenue at a low and a high price case and reports the difference as gross margin, annualised at 24 hours a day and 330 operating days a year.

Cut point: a diagnostic, not a driver

The dashboard reports a cut point of 367°C as a fixed, read-only figure. In the source model this number only feeds a display column. It does not change the product slate; the finished-product volumes come entirely from the per-stream split tables in the Data & Log tab. Treat the cut point as a reference for how well the split assumption matches a real distillation curve, not as a control that shifts output.

Why these numbers run about 39% above the source spreadsheet

The workbook this model was ported from has a cell reference error in its naphtha split: it reads the naphtha stream's LPG share instead of its naphtha share, which drops 88.59 m³/hr of naphtha from the output, 16.5% of everything fed to that step. That gap matches the workbook's own unexplained volume balance exactly. This dashboard fixes the reference and computes the naphtha split correctly, so margin runs about 39% above the original spreadsheet. Anyone comparing this dashboard's output against the source file should expect that gap and treat it as a correction, not a porting error.

Live prices and data sources

Three feeds load directly from this page, no server involved: Alberta's economic data service (WCS, WTI, WCS-WTI differential, monthly, no key), the EIA (daily crude and product spot prices), and Frankfurter (daily USD/CAD). If the EIA call fails, prices fall back to a snapshot file (fetch_prices.py), then to fixed reference prices.

Daily WCS is estimated as EIA's daily WTI minus Alberta's monthly WCS-WTI differential, since the differential moves slowly while the outright price tracks WTI day to day. Four of the seven products (LPG, naphtha, kerosene, diesel) have a public benchmark; the rest stay on the fixed reference price. Naphtha prices off finished gasoline, a blendstock proxy, not the same product. Diesel prices off US Gulf Coast ULSD, not the Alberta benchmark.

All differentials default to zero. At zero, live benchmark prices sit well off the fixed reference: LPG runs at roughly a third of it, naphtha roughly half again above it. Calibrate the differential fields in the Data & Log tab before treating a live price as more than indicative.

Scope and limitations

This engine prices one blend at one set of yields. It is volumetrically additive with no shrinkage correction on blending, and the LC-Fining step converts every product on a single generic density (862 kg/m³) rather than a per-product one, matching the source workbook's own simplification. Utilities, hydrogen, catalyst, labour, and capital charges sit outside the boundary; the reported figure is a gross crude-to-product margin, not a net cash margin. The 330 operating days a year is an availability assumption, not a measurement. Crude density and sulfur are fixed reference values from assay data, not live feeds.
